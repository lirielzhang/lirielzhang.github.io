Project 01

Xiaohongshu Headline Performance Analysis

An academic project about turning high-engagement Xiaohongshu examples into practical headline logic.

Content analytics Headline writing Prototype logic
What I wanted to know
Can high-engagement Xiaohongshu headlines be turned into a simple rule that content creators can reuse?
What I did
Worked with public Xiaohongshu post examples, cleaned the data, ranked posts by likes and favourites, then selected useful examples for headline prompt design.
What I found
Useful headline = clear benefit + specific context + matched content

A strong headline quickly tells users what they can eat, buy, visit, save, avoid or learn. It should be specific enough to judge, but still match the actual post.

香港本地人也爱的茶餐厅，猪排多士外脆里嫩！

likes · saves · comments

What I built

Input content → headline options

今天中午特意带小伙伴们来打卡一家我爱吃的茶餐厅。这家【炳记茶档】真的是香港排队王……

  1. 来香港一定要吃的茶餐厅！炳记猪排多士真的香
  2. 香港排队王茶档打卡｜猪排多士值得专门来吃吗？
  3. 第一次来香港别错过这家茶餐厅，猪排多士太上头
Next test: compare two headline versions for the same post and track saves, clicks and comments. The original demo is no longer live, so this portfolio shows the thinking and sample output instead.

Project 02

Slowood Social Content Audit

A self-initiated case based on public information, recent Instagram samples and Xiaohongshu search notes.

36KIG followers
1,030IG posts
15IG posts reviewed
15XHS notes coded
The problem
Slowood has an established audience and strong offline assets, but in the reviewed Instagram sample, visible comments were mostly near zero. The issue is not simply “more content needed”. It is about giving people a clearer reason to save, answer or share.

Slowood often says

  • Sustainability
  • Refill culture
  • Eco education
  • Store and service updates
Missing layer:
content that helps people decide

People often ask

  • What should I buy?
  • What smells good?
  • Where should I go?
  • Is it worth it?

What I saw in the data

Brand content leans educational. User content leans practical.

In the 15-post Instagram sample, education-led posts were the largest group. In the Xiaohongshu notes, recurring hooks included must-buy lists, fragrance/incense, store routes and negative or “worth it” reviews.

What I would make

Four content directions

First Slowood Basket

For first-time visitors who do not know what to buy first.

Slowood Scent Finder

For shoppers curious about fragrance but unsure what fits them.

Stop-by Moments

For people planning Central, airport or weekend routes.

One Small Swap

For turning refill education into a tiny action people can try.

How I would use it

Make one useful post, then test one thing.

For this first post, I would test whether a “starter basket” angle gets more saves than a general brand education post. I would track saves, shares, product questions and profile visits.

How I work

I don’t start with “what should we post?”

I start with what people already say. I look at search notes, reviews, saves, complaints and repeated questions. Then I write a brief, make one clear content piece and test one variable.

  • What are people trying to decide?
  • What makes them hesitate?
  • What would make this content worth saving?
